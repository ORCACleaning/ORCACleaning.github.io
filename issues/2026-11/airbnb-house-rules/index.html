THE PLATFORMS · AIRBNB HOUSE RULES

Airbnb restricts checkout chores: what hosts need to know

Last reviewed: 18 November 2026

For bookings made from 20 November 2026, Airbnb's new policy generally prohibits demanding substantial checkout chores, including stripping beds and washing dishes. Here is what remains acceptable and how hosts can prepare.

What changed
Airbnb's new House Rules policy applies to reservations booked on or after 20 November 2026.
Who it affects
hosts and co-hosts with Airbnb listings.
What to do
review your House Rules, remove any checkout chores that no longer comply, and check your turnover arrangements.

On 20 October 2026 Airbnb told hosts it was changing what they may ask of guests at checkout. The policy is published on Airbnb's Australian Help Centre and applies to reservations booked from 20 November 2026.

What guests can no longer be asked to do. Airbnb says hosts generally cannot require tasks that take significant time or effort, or that "would typically be performed by a cleaner". Its examples include washing and putting away dishes, removing sheets or starting a load of laundry, taking rubbish off-site, and avoiding the use of an amenity the listing offers. Airbnb may allow exceptions where a listing's circumstances or relevant local requirements justify a particular rule.

What you can still ask. Airbnb lists simple requests that remain acceptable: putting used dishes in the sink, gathering used towels, sorting rubbish and recycling into the indoor bins, and turning off the air conditioning when leaving. Rules must be reasonable and set out in the listing's House Rules section before booking.

How Airbnb will enforce it. Airbnb says it will begin reviewing listings on 20 November, with reviews running through March 2027 and continuing after that. It will notify hosts by email and in the app if their rules need attention. Affected hosts will have at least 20 days to update the rules or appeal, and one appeal is allowed. If a rule remains after an unsuccessful appeal, Airbnb may display a notice on the listing and take further action, including reduced search visibility. Hosts generally cannot impose their own fines or penalties for House Rule breaches, although Airbnb permits disclosure of certain fines imposed by local authorities or community associations.

What now lands on the host. From 20 November, a guest who books can leave without stripping a bed, washing a cup or taking a bag of rubbish out. Those jobs do not disappear. Depending on what guests leave behind, the host or turnover team may need to strip used beds, remove pillowcases, wash, dry and put away dishes, glasses and pans, empty bins and remove rubbish, alongside the usual cleaning, bed-making and restocking. With back-to-back bookings, all of it has to fit between one guest's checkout and the next guest's check-in, and in a busy summer that can happen several times a week. Whether you do your own turnovers or use a cleaner, hosts should ensure these tasks are covered in their turnover arrangements where needed, allowing enough time before the next arrival.

Separate contracts are restricted. Airbnb permits them only in limited circumstances, including certain legal or insurance requirements, community-association rules, monthly-stay rental agreements, Airbnb Luxe listings and agreements covering some optional amenities. Where permitted, contracts must be uploaded through Airbnb's listing settings. Airbnb says it does not require guests to sign them or help enforce them.

What this means for WA hosts

Bottom line: for bookings from 20 November, substantial checkout chores can no longer be required of Airbnb guests.

Who it applies to: Airbnb hosts and co-hosts.

Do this:

  1. Review your listing's House Rules and remove any checkout chores that no longer comply, such as stripping beds, doing laundry, washing or putting away dishes, or taking rubbish off-site.
  2. Replace them with requests Airbnb still allows: dishes in the sink, towels gathered, rubbish sorted into the indoor bins, air conditioning off.
  3. Make sure every rule you rely on is set out in the listing's House Rules section before guests book.
  4. Check your cleaning scope of work and turnover times for the peak season.
  5. Watch your email and the app for review notices; if you receive one, you have at least 20 days to update or appeal.

Applies to: bookings made from 20 November 2026.
