ASK WESTERN HOST · THE REGISTER

Renting, granny flats and the 90-night rule

Last reviewed: 17 November 2026

Three common short-stay registration questions, answered using the WA Government's official guidance. This month: operating from a rental, counting the Perth 90-night exemption, and registering a granny flat. _These questions are adapted from topics in the WA STRA Register's official FAQ; they are not submissions from individual readers. Send us yours for next month._

I rent the property. Can I register and run a short-stay from it?

Possibly. The register's guidance is that a tenant may be able to offer the property, or part of it, as a short stay where the tenancy agreement does not prohibit it, as a subletting arrangement. Check your tenancy agreement and obtain any consent required for the subletting; the register advises tenants to discuss the arrangement with their landlord or property manager. If you go ahead, you as the operator register the premises. Registration is a separate requirement and does not override the conditions of your tenancy agreement, so get the paperwork right before you list. Full text: wa.gov.au STRA register FAQ.

When does my 12 months start for the 90 nights?

From your registration date, not 1 January. The Perth-metro exemption lets an unhosted property run without development approval where it is "leased for 90 nights or less (non-consecutive) within a 12-month period, as per the date of registration." So if you registered in March, your 90-night year runs March to March. Two things make the counting fairer than it first looks. Only nights actually let to guests count: the register "will capture the number of nights a registered premises is booked," and "Periods when the property is blocked out by an owner for private use are not counted towards nights booked." The register monitors reported bookings and sends notifications at 80 and 90 nights booked. Keep your own accurate records too, including direct bookings, which must be entered manually. Your registration itself also expires 12 months after it is completed, so the renewal and the exemption year run together. Above 90 let nights, you need development approval. Full text: wa.gov.au STRA register FAQ.

Do I need to register a granny flat, or a room in my own home?

Yes, if you are letting it short-term. The register is clear that "It is now mandatory for all STRA to be registered," whether it is a whole house, a granny flat or a room. What changes is the category. A room you let while living on site is hosted STRA, which is exempt from planning approval across WA but still has to be registered. A granny flat may qualify as hosted where the host lives in the main house, and the reverse can also qualify, where the host lives in the granny flat and lets the main house. If both dwellings are used for short stays, the register treats them as unhosted and each must be registered separately. Register each one you let, and keep each registration number on its own listing. Full text: wa.gov.au STRA register FAQ.
