CASE FILE · THE FIRST FINE

The missing number behind WA's first fine

Last reviewed: 17 November 2026

In August, Consumer Protection announced its first A$2,000 infringement under WA's short-stay laws. The allegation concerned a missing registration number. We look at what the public record confirms, what it does not, and the checks every advertiser can make.

What happened
a provider was issued a A$2,000 infringement for allegedly advertising a property without its registration number.
Who it affects
anyone advertising STRA in WA, on any platform.
What to do
show the correct registration number, conspicuously, in every advertisement.

What Consumer Protection confirmed

On 21 August 2026 the WA Government announced the first infringement under the state's short-stay laws: A$2,000, issued by Consumer Protection to a short-stay provider. It related to a residential property in the Perth metropolitan area that was allegedly advertised without its STRA registration number. The Commissioner for Consumer Protection, Trish Blake, said the case "demonstrates that Consumer Protection is actively enforcing legislation", and that the agency would keep monitoring compliance.

What the announcement did not establish

The announcement did not name the provider, the suburb or the platform, and neither do we. It reported that an infringement notice was issued; it did not report whether the notice was paid, challenged or decided by a court. What it shows is the regulator's priority, not a court's finding.

Which advertising obligations apply

Every advertisement for a registered short stay must show the correct registration number in a readily visible way. Booking platforms and advertising publishers carry their own obligations under the regime, including restrictions on publishing advertisements for unregistered premises and advertisements with a missing or falsely displayed number. Depending on the circumstances, a non-compliant advertisement can expose both the provider and the publisher to regulatory consequences. Certain offences under the STRA legislation carry maximum court-imposed fines of A$20,000 for individuals and A$100,000 for corporations. These are statutory ceilings, not the amount of the infringement notice issued in this case.

What a host or property manager can check this week

  • Every live listing (Airbnb, Booking.com, Stayz, your own website, social posts) shows the registration number where a reader can see it.
  • The number is the correct one for that exact property, not copied from another listing.
  • Listings still show it after any platform redesign or listing edit.
  • The public STRA Register confirms the property is registered and the number is valid. That confirms registration only; planning approval and other requirements still need checking separately.

The reported A$2,000 infringement is a reminder that a small advertising omission can carry a significant regulatory consequence.

"This first infringement demonstrates that Consumer Protection is actively enforcing legislation."Trish Blake, Commissioner for Consumer Protection.

The case at a glance

  • The infringement: A$2,000 notice announced by Consumer Protection on 21 August 2026.
  • The allegation: an advertisement for a Perth metropolitan property did not display its registration number.
  • Not reported: the provider's identity, the platform, or any later outcome.
  • The rule: the correct number, readily visible, in every advertisement.
  • The ceiling: maximum court-imposed fines for certain offences of A$20,000 (individual) and A$100,000 (corporation), not the infringement amount.
Sources: wa.gov.au; consumerprotection.wa.gov.au; legislation.wa.gov.au
