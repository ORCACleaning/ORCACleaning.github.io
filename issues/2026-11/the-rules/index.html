THE RULES · WA SHORT-STAY REGISTER

WA's short-stay rules now carry real penalties

Last reviewed: 17 November 2026

WA's first short-stay infringement arrived in August. From registration numbers to planning approvals and renewals, here are five developments worth checking before Perth's summer booking season.

What changed
Consumer Protection has begun issuing fines, and the 1 January 2026 rules are now in force.
Who it affects
every hosted and unhosted short-term rental provider in WA.
What to do
confirm your registration is current and your number is shown in every advertisement.

Western Australia's short-stay framework has stopped being a form-filling exercise and started producing penalties, published registers and council decisions. For a host, the practical question each month is narrow: is anything new that I have to act on? Here are the five developments that matter most right now, newest first, each with the one thing to do about it.

First infringement, 21 August 2026. Consumer Protection announced its first A$2,000 infringement under the short-stay laws, over an advertisement for a Perth metropolitan home that allegedly did not show a registration number (full story, page 22). Open every live listing and check your number is visible and correct.

Anyone can check a listing. The STRA Register's public check tool lets you confirm whether an address is registered or a number is valid. Regulator: Department of Local Government, Industry Regulation and Safety. It covers all WA. Use it to verify a property before you co-host, sublet or buy, and to confirm your own entry reads correctly.

Planning compliance under scrutiny. Since 1 January 2026, providers must be able to demonstrate that their properties meet applicable development-approval requirements to remain registered. The obligation to register before advertising has applied since 1 January 2025. Certain offences carry maximum court-imposed fines of A$20,000 for an individual and A$100,000 for a corporation. If you operate unhosted accommodation, check your local planning requirements rather than assuming registration alone is enough.

Councils are applying their own local rules. The City of Fremantle's unhosted policy is now live, with its scheme change, Amendment 88, gazetted on 9 January 2026; the City of Mandurah's Amendment 8 is progressing through the Western Australian Planning Commission. These are local-government planning rules that sit on top of state registration, and they differ from council to council. Check your own council's position before you take unhosted bookings beyond the exemption, because a state registration does not settle the planning question on its own.

Renewals run on your own date. A registration expires 12 months after it is completed, so every property renews on its own anniversary, not a common date. The register sends reminders as expiry approaches, and renewal costs A$100. A lapsed registration cannot lawfully advertise or take bookings, so make sure the reminder email reaches an inbox someone reads, and renew before the date passes.

"Displaying a registration number is not optional."Trish Blake, Commissioner for Consumer Protection.

WA rules at a glance

  • Registration has been mandatory for all hosted and unhosted STRA since 1 January 2025.
  • Fees: A$250 initial registration, A$100 yearly renewal (GST status not stated on the official fees page).
  • Your registration number must be displayed in a conspicuous way in every advertisement.
  • Perth metro: unhosted STRA is generally exempt from development approval for up to 90 let nights in the 12-month period counted from registration. Operating beyond that needs planning approval; there is no general statewide 90-night operating cap.
  • Hosted STRA (the host lives on site) is exempt from planning approval across WA, but still must register.
  • Maximum court-imposed fines for certain offences: A$20,000 (individual), A$100,000 (corporation).

What this means for WA hosts

Bottom line: enforcement has started, so the simplest immediate check is to be visibly compliant before an inspector looks.

Who it applies to: every host, owner, co-host and property manager running STRA in WA.

Actions:

  • Check every live listing shows your current registration number, clearly.
  • Confirm your registration has not lapsed; renew for A$100 if it is near expiry.
  • Confirm your planning position if you run unhosted and may exceed 90 nights.
  • Verify any property you co-host or manage on the public STRA Register.

Deadline: before your next booking goes live.

Cost: A$100 renewal if due (GST not stated).

Link: wa.gov.au/organisation/register
