PRACTICAL TOOLKIT · SUMMER READY

A 25-point check before Perth's peak season

Last reviewed: 17 November 2026

Perth's busiest short-stay months run from December to February, on hot days, full pools and a live bushfire season. This one-page audit sorts the legal requirements from good practice, with the WA source for each.

7 min read

What to check
25 items across safety, guest comfort, your listing and your neighbours, before the summer rush.
Who it affects
Every WA host, owner, co-host and property manager running a residential short stay.
What to do
Work the list once now, fix anything marked Law first, and keep the sheet with your property file.

As the summer holidays approach, short-stay operators face tighter turnarounds and less room for a forgotten detail. A dead smoke alarm battery, a pool gate that no longer self-latches, or a listing missing its registration number are the kind of small gaps that are cheap to fix in November and expensive to discover in January.

This audit is built to be printed on one page and worked through room by room. It separates two very different things. Some items are the law in Western Australia, and the obligation is usually triggered the moment a dwelling is rented or made available for hire, which is the category that captures short-stay letting. Others are good practice: not legally required of a short stay as such, but the difference between a smooth season and a bad review or a worse incident.

Where an item is marked Law, we name the WA regulator and the page it comes from, all read on 17 November 2026. Where the law is genuinely unclear for short stays, we say so and treat the item as good practice. None of this replaces advice from the regulator or your local government, and the detailed technical rules (exact fence heights, alarm placement, ventilation openings) sit in the regulators' own guides. Start with the items marked Law, then work the rest.

One habit worth keeping: date and sign the sheet each time. A dated check records what you inspected and what work is still outstanding.

Key point: A dated pre-season checklist records what was inspected, shows what work is outstanding and helps you book professional checks where required.

— Fix these first

Verify smoke alarms (items 1 to 3), RCDs (item 4), any electrical and gas work (item 5), pool and spa barriers (items 6 and 7) and your STRA registration number on every advertisement (item 19). Book licensed trades or inspections where needed, and book them now rather than in the first week of December. Then complete the guest-comfort and neighbour checks.

What this means for WA hosts

Bottom line: Clear the legal items first, then work the comfort and neighbour items before December.

Who it applies to: Every WA host, owner, co-host and property manager running a residential short stay.

Do this:

  1. Book a licensed electrician and gas fitter for any safety-switch, wiring or gas work.
  2. Confirm your pool or spa barrier is compliant and its council inspection is current.
  3. Put your STRA registration number on every listing and advertisement.
  4. Test alarms, A/C and Wi-Fi, and restock linen to three sets per bed.
  5. Date and sign the sheet, and keep it with your property file.

Deadline / cost: Complete before peak season (December to February). Costs vary by trade; the audit itself is free.

Official link: wa.gov.au (Building and Energy) and dfes.wa.gov.au. Check pool rules with your local government.
